Who does what

A cook and a bartender open the same app and see a different one. Two things decide what each person gets: how senior they are, and which department they work in.

Two questions decide what someone sees

↓ Down the board · level

How much do they decide?

Doing the work, leading a shift, running a department or running the outlet. This sets what they can approve and whose work they can see.

→ Across the board · department

What is their work?

Kitchen, bar, housekeeping and so on. This sets their shifts, their checklists, what they make, the store they use and the recipes they read.

So a cook and a bartender share a level but not a job. Each sits on the same rung of a different ladder.

The outlet, department by department

Tap any two jobs to compare them below. On a phone, swipe the board sideways.

Supplies: a department asks for items and quantities only; the Main Store's Store Keeper picks the supplier and the delivery date, then receives, and the department is told at each step. Bills: whoever receives an order adds its bill; bills for services (linen, pest control, repairs) are added under Stock → Bills; the GM sees every bill. Above the outlet: the Area Manager sees and approves across the outlets in the area; the Account Owner reads every outlet. HR Admin, Security Admin and the Auditor work company-wide. Server, Cashier and Host on the floor are from Test Bar 3.0, a standalone bar.

Same level, different job

What each level adds

Does the work

Their own shifts, checklists and tasks; what their department makes, from its recipes; the store their job uses, if any.

Cook, Commis, Bartender, Room Attendant, Front Desk Executive, Technician

Leads a shift

Adds the department's roster and attendance, giving tasks to the team, and counting the department's store.

Sous Chef, Head Bartender, Captain, Housekeeping Supervisor, Bell Captain

Runs a department

Adds building and publishing the roster, first approval of leave and swaps and of unusual supply requests, the store's prices and costs, editing checklists and assigning repairs.

Executive Chef, Bar Manager, Executive Housekeeper, Front Office Manager, Chief Engineer

Runs the outlet

Adds every department, the outlet's sales and costs, people cost, approving unusual supply requests (and hearing of every one), and the outlet's own user admin.

General Manager, Assistant General Manager; the Bar Manager of a standalone bar

What everyone has, whatever the job

  • My shifts and clock-inTheir own roster, clocking in on site, and swaps.
  • LeaveRequests and balances, approved up their own department's chain.
  • TasksTheir checklists and one-off tasks, and reporting a problem for repair.
  • To do listWhat waits for them: approvals, jobs to assign, supplies to order or receive.
  • My weekTheir hours, on-time record and tasks. No one else's figures.
Built from the demo company's own setup files. Each customer names its own departments and job titles when it is set up, and chooses what each job gets by default; one person can also be given a second job's access on top.
